The Greats

Scientists Who
Changed the World

Throughout history, scientists have made discoveries that transformed our understanding of nature and improved human life across the globe.

Introduction

Curious Minds, Lasting Impact

The history of science is a story of extraordinary individuals who dared to question the world around them. Armed with curiosity, rigor, and persistence, these scientists built the foundation of our modern understanding — from the laws governing motion and gravity to the mysteries of life and the structure of matter.

Here we celebrate six scientists whose work continues to shape science, technology, and everyday life today.

Profiles

Meet the Scientists

Six remarkable individuals whose discoveries changed everything.

Physics & Mathematics

Isaac Newton

English physicist and mathematician who laid the foundations of classical mechanics.

  • Laws of Motion — describing how forces affect objects
  • Universal gravitation — explaining why objects fall
  • Optics — discovering that white light contains a spectrum of colours
  • Co-inventor of calculus (independently of Leibniz)

Biology & Natural Science

Charles Darwin

British naturalist who developed the theory of evolution by natural selection.

  • Theory of evolution by natural selection
  • Published On the Origin of Species (1859)
  • Showed how species adapt to their environment over generations
  • Revolutionised our understanding of the diversity of life

Physics & Chemistry

Marie Curie

Polish-French physicist and chemist who pioneered research on radioactivity.

  • Pioneering research on radioactivity
  • Discovered the elements Polonium and Radium
  • First woman to win a Nobel Prize
  • Only person to win Nobel Prizes in two different sciences

Theoretical Physics

Albert Einstein

German-born theoretical physicist who developed the theory of relativity and made major contributions to modern physics.

  • Special and General Theory of Relativity
  • Famous equation: E = mc²
  • Nobel Prize in Physics (1921) for the photoelectric effect
  • Contributions to quantum mechanics and cosmology

Physics — India's Nobel Laureate

C. V. Raman ⭐

Indian physicist Chandrasekhara Venkata Raman made a landmark discovery in the study of light scattering.

  • Raman Effect — scattering of light with a change in wavelength
  • Nobel Prize in Physics (1930) — first Indian scientist to win
  • Contributions to the study of optics and crystal dynamics
  • Founded the Indian Journal of Physics

Physics & Plant Biology

Jagadish Chandra Bose

Indian scientist who made pioneering contributions to both radio science and plant biology.

  • Pioneered research in radio and microwave optics
  • Demonstrated that plants respond to stimuli
  • Invented the crescograph to measure plant growth
  • One of the first scientists to investigate radio waves in the GHz range
