Science Subject

Chemistry: The Science
of Matter

Chemistry is the study of matter, its properties, composition, and the changes it undergoes. Everything around us is made of matter — and chemistry helps us understand it.

Introduction

What is Chemistry?

Chemistry is the branch of science concerned with the substances that make up matter, their properties, and the reactions they undergo. It bridges the gap between physics and biology, helping explain both the structure of the universe and the processes of life.

From the water we drink to the medicines we take, chemistry is at the heart of almost everything around us. By understanding chemistry, we gain insight into how the world is built at the smallest possible scale.

Structure

Atoms — The Building Blocks

An atom is the basic unit of an element. Every atom consists of three types of particles.

p+

Protons

Found in the nucleus. Protons carry a positive electric charge. The number of protons determines which element an atom is.

n

Neutrons

Also found in the nucleus. Neutrons carry no electric charge. They add mass to the atom.

e-

Electrons

Electrons orbit the nucleus in shells. They carry a negative electric charge and are involved in chemical bonding.

Reactions

Chemical Reactions

A chemical reaction occurs when substances change into new substances with different properties.

Oxidation

Rusting of Iron

Iron reacts with oxygen and water in the air to form iron oxide (rust). This is a slow, irreversible chemical change.

Combustion

Burning of Wood

Wood reacts with oxygen during burning (combustion), releasing heat energy, light, carbon dioxide, and water vapour.

Biochemistry

Digestion of Food

Enzymes in our digestive system break down complex food molecules into simpler ones through chemical reactions.

Denaturation

Cooking an Egg

Heat causes the proteins in an egg to unfold and re-bond in a new structure — transforming a liquid into a solid.

Safety First

Chemistry Safety

When working with chemicals or laboratory equipment, safety is always the top priority.

⚠ Important Safety Rules

  • Always wear appropriate safety equipment — safety goggles and gloves when required.
  • Never taste or smell chemicals directly from a container.
  • Do not mix unknown substances — unexpected reactions can be dangerous.
  • Always follow your teacher's or supervisor's instructions.
  • Handle all laboratory equipment carefully and report any breakages immediately.

🥇 Fun Fact

The chemical symbol for gold is Au, which comes from the Latin word aurum. Many element symbols come from their Latin names — for example, Fe (iron) from ferrum, and Na (sodium) from natrium.
