Physics: Understanding
How Things Work

Physics is the branch of science that studies matter, energy, motion, forces, and their interactions — from a falling ball to the movement of planets.

Introduction

What is Physics?

Physics is the branch of science that explores the fundamental principles governing the natural world. It studies matter — everything that has mass and takes up space — and energy, which is the ability to do work. Physics also looks at motion, forces, and how these interact with each other.

Without physics, we would not understand why objects fall, how electricity flows, why the sky is blue, or how bridges stay standing. Physics helps us understand everything from the smallest subatomic particles to vast cosmic structures.

Forces

Force and Motion

A force is a push or pull that can change the motion of an object.

🚪

Pushing a Door

Applying a push force causes the door to rotate about its hinges and swing open.

⚽

Kicking a Football

A kick applies a force that launches the football forward, giving it speed and direction.

🪣

Pulling a Drawer

Pulling exerts a force that overcomes friction and slides the drawer out smoothly.

🌍

Gravity

Gravity is a pulling force that the Earth exerts on all objects, drawing them towards its centre.

Energy

Forms of Energy

Energy is the ability to do work. It exists in many forms and can be converted from one type to another.

🏃

Kinetic Energy

The energy of an object in motion. A moving car or a flying ball both possess kinetic energy.

⬆️

Potential Energy

Stored energy due to position or condition. A ball held up high has gravitational potential energy.

🔥

Heat Energy

The energy transferred due to a difference in temperature, such as from a flame to a pan.

💡

Light Energy

Energy that travels as electromagnetic waves, allowing us to see the world around us.

⚡

Electrical Energy

Energy carried by moving electric charges, powering our homes, devices, and machines.

🧪

Chemical Energy

Energy stored in chemical bonds, released during reactions such as burning or digestion.

Optics

Light

Light is a form of energy that allows us to see objects. It travels in straight lines at approximately 300,000 kilometres per second — the fastest speed in the universe.

Important concepts in the study of light include:

  • Reflection — Light bouncing off a surface, such as a mirror.
  • Refraction — The bending of light when it passes through different materials.
  • Dispersion — The splitting of white light into a spectrum of colours by a prism.
  • Shadows — Formed when an opaque object blocks light.
  • Lenses — Curved glass that focuses or spreads light rays.
Acoustics

Sound

Sound is produced by vibrations. When an object vibrates, it creates pressure waves in the surrounding medium — these waves are what we hear as sound.

Sound needs a medium to travel through. It can travel through:

  • Air — at approximately 343 m/s at room temperature.
  • Water — nearly five times faster than in air.
  • Solids — even faster, because particles are closer together.

Unlike light, sound cannot travel through a vacuum. This is why there is no sound in space.

Real World

Physics in Everyday Life

Physics concepts are all around us in daily life. Here are six clear examples.

Physics Concept Everyday Example
Gravity Objects falling to the ground
Friction Brakes stopping a bicycle
Electricity Operating household appliances
Reflection Seeing yourself in a mirror
Refraction A straw appearing bent in water
Sound Music from a speaker

⚡ Fun Fact

Light travels at approximately 300,000 km/s — far faster than the speed of sound (343 m/s). This is why we always see lightning before we hear thunder, even though both happen at exactly the same moment during a storm.
