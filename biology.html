Biology: The Science
of Life

Biology is the study of living organisms — humans, animals, plants, and microorganisms — and their interactions with the environment.

Introduction

What is Biology?

Biology explores the living world in all its complexity. From the tiniest bacteria to the largest whale, from rainforests to the human immune system, biology helps us understand what it means to be alive.

By studying biology, we learn how bodies work, how species evolve, how ecosystems are maintained, and how we can protect the biodiversity that sustains all life on Earth.

Cells

The Cell — Basic Unit of Life

The cell is the basic structural and functional unit of all living organisms. There are two major types of cells.

🌿

Plant Cells

Plant cells have a rigid cell wall, chloroplasts for photosynthesis, and a large central vacuole for storing water and nutrients.

🐾

Animal Cells

Animal cells lack a cell wall and chloroplasts. They have a flexible cell membrane and smaller vacuoles.

Important Cell Parts and Their Functions

Cell Part Function
Nucleus Controls all cell activities and contains the genetic material (DNA)
Cell Membrane Controls the movement of substances in and out of the cell
Cytoplasm Jelly-like fluid that fills the cell; site of many chemical reactions
Mitochondria Releases energy from food through respiration (the "powerhouse" of the cell)
Ribosomes Help manufacture proteins that the cell needs to function
Chloroplast Carries out photosynthesis in plant cells; contains green chlorophyll
Vacuole Stores water, nutrients, and waste products within the cell
Plants

Photosynthesis

Photosynthesis is the remarkable process by which green plants use sunlight to produce their own food. It is the foundation of almost all food chains on Earth.

The Process Uses:

  • Sunlight — energy source from the Sun
  • Carbon Dioxide (CO₂) — absorbed through tiny pores in leaves
  • Water (H₂O) — absorbed through the roots
  • Chlorophyll — the green pigment that captures light

Output: The process produces glucose (food) and releases oxygen — the gas we breathe.

Equation: CO₂ + H₂O + Light → Glucose + O₂

Human Biology

The Human Body

Our body is made up of many complex, interconnected organ systems that work together to keep us alive and healthy.

🍎

Digestive System

Breaks down food into nutrients that can be absorbed into the bloodstream and used by the body.

🫁

Respiratory System

Takes oxygen from the air into the lungs and removes carbon dioxide from the body.

❤️

Circulatory System

The heart pumps blood through vessels to deliver oxygen and nutrients to every cell in the body.

🧠

Nervous System

The brain, spinal cord, and nerves control body functions and process information from the senses.

🦴

Skeletal System

The skeleton gives our body structure, protects internal organs, and allows movement via muscles and joints.

Biodiversity

The Variety of Life on Earth

Biodiversity refers to the variety of living organisms found on our planet — from microscopic bacteria to giant sequoia trees.

Kingdom Plantae

Plants

From mosses to oak trees, plants form the base of most food chains and produce the oxygen that sustains nearly all life.

Kingdom Animalia

Animals

Animals are multi-cellular organisms that consume food for energy. They range from tiny insects to massive blue whales.

Kingdom Fungi

Fungi

Fungi decompose dead organic matter, recycling nutrients back into the soil and playing a crucial role in ecosystems.

🌍 Protect Biodiversity

Every species plays a role in maintaining healthy ecosystems. Habitat loss, pollution, and climate change threaten biodiversity worldwide. Protecting nature means protecting ourselves — a healthy planet supports all life, including our own.
