Introduction

What is Space Science?

Space science — also called astronomy — is the study of celestial objects and phenomena that lie beyond Earth's atmosphere. Scientists observe planets, moons, stars, galaxies, asteroids, and comets to understand the origin, structure, and future of the universe.

From the first telescopes pointed at the night sky to powerful spacecraft sent to explore Mars and beyond, humanity's curiosity about the cosmos has driven some of the greatest scientific achievements in history.

Our Solar System

The Eight Planets

Our solar system consists of the Sun and the eight planets that orbit it, along with moons, asteroids, and comets.

1st from Sun · Rocky

Mercury

The smallest planet and closest to the Sun. Extreme temperature swings between day and night.

2nd from Sun · Rocky

Venus

The hottest planet despite not being closest to the Sun, due to its thick atmosphere.

3rd from Sun · Rocky

Earth

Our home — the only planet known to support life, with liquid water and a breathable atmosphere.

4th from Sun · Rocky

Mars

Known as the Red Planet due to iron oxide on its surface. Home to the largest volcano in the solar system.

5th from Sun · Gas Giant

Jupiter

The largest planet in our solar system. Its famous Great Red Spot is a storm larger than Earth.

6th from Sun · Gas Giant

Saturn

Famous for its spectacular ring system made of ice and rock particles orbiting the planet.

7th from Sun · Ice Giant

Uranus

Rotates on its side — its axis is tilted almost 98°. It appears blue-green due to methane in its atmosphere.

8th from Sun · Ice Giant

Neptune

The farthest planet, with the strongest winds in the solar system — reaching 2,100 km/h.

Earth's Satellite

The Moon

The Moon is Earth's only natural satellite and the fifth-largest moon in the solar system. It does not produce its own light — instead, it reflects sunlight from the Sun.

The Moon completes one full orbit around Earth approximately every 27.3 days, and its changing positions relative to the Earth and Sun create the familiar cycle of phases — new moon, crescent, quarter, gibbous, and full moon.

Gravitational pull from the Moon causes the ocean tides on Earth.

Stellar Science

Stars

Stars are enormous balls of hot gas — mostly hydrogen and helium — held together by gravity. Deep in their cores, nuclear fusion converts hydrogen into helium, releasing vast amounts of energy as heat and light.

Our Sun is the closest star to Earth, located about 150 million kilometres away. The next nearest star, Proxima Centauri, is about 4.2 light-years distant — so far that its light takes over four years to reach us.

Stars come in different sizes, temperatures, and colours, from cool red dwarfs to blazing blue supergiants.

Mission Control

Space Exploration

Humanity has sent both astronauts and robotic spacecraft to explore many parts of our solar system — a remarkable achievement for a species that only left Earth for the first time in 1961.

Space exploration helps us learn more about:

  • Other planets and their atmospheres
  • The Moon — its geology and history
  • Asteroids and comets
  • The Sun — its energy, solar flares, and magnetic field
  • The origin and age of the universe

Missions like NASA's Voyager probes, the Mars rovers, and the James Webb Space Telescope continue to revolutionise our understanding of space.
